# Muon lifetime and relativistic time dilation


## The muon

The muon is an elementary particle (a heavier cousin of the electron) with a mean
lifetime of $2.1969811 \pm 0.0000022$ microseconds. It decays via the weak force into an
electron, an electron antineutrino, and a muon neutrino. The half-life is approximately
1.52 microseconds.


## Origin: cosmic ray air showers

Cosmic ray protons hit nitrogen and oxygen nuclei in the upper atmosphere at roughly 15 km
altitude. The collisions produce pions, which decay almost instantly (26 ns half-life) into
muons. The muons travel downward toward the surface at speeds near $c$ (typically
$0.995c$--$0.999c$).


## The puzzle

At 2.2 microseconds mean lifetime, a muon traveling at $0.998c$ covers about 660 meters
per half-life in the lab frame. From 15 km altitude, the survival probability after
$15000/660 \approx 23$ half-lives would be $2^{-23} \approx 10^{-7}$. Essentially none
should reach sea level.

Yet we detect approximately 10,000 muons per square meter per minute at sea level.


## Resolution: special relativity

**Lab frame (time dilation):** The muon's internal clock runs slow by the Lorentz factor
$\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$. For $v = 0.998c$, $\gamma \approx 15$. For higher-energy
muons ($v = 0.9995c$), $\gamma \approx 30$--50. The effective lifetime in the lab frame is
$\gamma \times 2.2$ microseconds $\approx 30$--100 microseconds --- long enough to traverse
15 km.

**Muon frame (length contraction):** The muon sees the atmosphere Lorentz-contracted by the
same factor $\gamma$. The 15 km becomes $15000/\gamma \approx 300$--1000 meters --- short
enough to cross in one or two half-lives.

Both frames agree on the outcome: the muon reaches the ground. They disagree on whether
time stretched or distance shrank.


## Significance

This is one of the cleanest experimental confirmations of special relativity. The
detection rate of cosmic-ray muons at sea level quantitatively matches the relativistic
prediction and is incompatible with Newtonian mechanics. The muon lifetime experiment is a
standard undergraduate physics lab: stop cosmic-ray muons in a scintillator block, measure
the time between arrival and decay electron emission, histogram thousands of events, and
fit the exponential.
